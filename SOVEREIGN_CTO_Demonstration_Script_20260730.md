## Screen 1 — Home Dashboard: the opening claim, shown, not just stated

**Persona:** PROGRAM_MANAGER (or similar non-admin role with real program data).

**Say:** "This is what a program manager sees first — not a feature list, a real work
surface." Point to Program Health's five programs, the Dependency Health Index, and
Learning Velocity metric boxes.

**Do:** Click into one program tile. Show the point-of-contact name and per-tile
variance. **Talking point:** "This isn't a mockup — every one of these numbers is
computed live from the same underlying obligation data the platform tracks, and we've
specifically hardened the math behind this screen after finding and fixing a real
calculation bug here in our own testing." (This is the fabrication/self-correction
story's first, soft mention — full version comes in Screen 4.)

**Watch for:** confirm no program shows an obviously wrong percentage before you're in
the room. This exact screen is where WH-34 was originally found — it's fixed, but it's
also exactly the kind of thing a numerically literate evaluator would check by eye.

## Screen 2 — The classification boundary (GD-10): shown, not described

**Persona:** any role that surfaces the banner (APEX, ARIA, or FLOWPATH all display it).

**Say:** "This platform processes unclassified data only. That's not a policy statement
— it's enforced." Point to the on-screen banner directly.

**Do, if time and a real control exist to demonstrate this:** attempt to submit or flag
something as a higher classification and show the block and the resulting log entry.
**If no live control exists for this**, describe it plainly rather than fake it — the
Read-Ahead's own discipline about not manufacturing polish applies here specifically.

## Screen 3 — The three-role governed workflow

**Persona switches required:** ANALYST (or PROGRAM_MANAGER) → COMPLIANCE_OFFICER.

**Do:** In SCRIBE, show a drafted document. Switch personas to COMPLIANCE_OFFICER, open
ARIA's CLEAR Certification Queue, certify the same item. Open TRACER, trace the chain
back from the certified item to its governing regulation.

**Say:** "Draft, certify, trace — three different roles, one real, unbroken chain. No
AI model makes the certification decision. It evaluates fixed rules against fixed
inputs and a named human certifies."

---

## Screen 4 — Reliability evidence: the strongest beat in the room

**Say, plainly, without hedging:** "We don't just claim this platform is reliable — here's
how we know." Walk through, in order:

1. **The fifty-one-screen comprehensive audit** — zero MAJOR or BROKEN findings.
2. **Walkthrough H Parts 4 and 6** — this platform's oldest, most load-bearing claim,
   run live in a real browser, held completely.
3. **The self-correction record — tell this story directly, don't bury it.** "During
   this same development arc, our own verification process caught a fabricated status
   report before it entered the permanent record, and separately caught a
   double-counted test figure that had been presented as reconciled. Both were
   corrected publicly, with the original flawed versions kept in our history rather
   than deleted." **This is a deliberate choice to lead with a real mistake as
   evidence, not a risk to manage around** — a process that only ever reports clean
   results is less credible than one that's been tested by a real failure and caught
   it.

**This is the natural place for WH-43's live confirmation, if you have it — see the
branch below.**

## The WH-43 branch point

**If WH-43 was confirmed live before the demonstration:** say so directly here, as the
capstone of the reliability beat: "The one item that remained open even after everything
above — a live check that a workflow queue's count matches what a separate review panel
shows — we confirmed ourselves, live, before walking in today." You can choose to
literally repeat the check in the room (open NEXUS's Travel & Time Queue, open the
Reviewer's Workspace, show the two numbers match) — this is a strong, concrete moment
if you have the confidence to do it live, and a real evaluator will likely respect the
willingness to demonstrate rather than just assert it.

**If WH-43 was NOT confirmed before the demonstration:** do not claim it was. Say
plainly: "One specific item is still open as of today — a live count-matching check
between two screens that show related information. We've fixed the underlying code and
tested it, but haven't yet confirmed it live, and we're not going to claim we have."
This is exactly the kind of named, bounded gap the whole reliability narrative has been
built around crediting rather than hiding — the same discipline applies to your own
talking points as to the platform's documentation.

## Screen 6 — PPBE, the full proof of concept

**Persona:** ANALYST for ARC specifically — confirmed as the correct gating role.

**Do:** Open ARC. Select a program with a real seeded exhibit (ALPHA) — show the context
panel, the CLEAR certification status. Select a program without one (BRAVO, CHARLIE, or
DELTA) — show the honest "no seeded exhibit" disclosure. **Say:** "This is deliberately
honest, not polished away — where we don't have real data yet, the platform says so
rather than fabricate it."

**Do:** Model a regulatory change, show the projected impact report, and the routing
recommendation buttons. **Say, before clicking:** "These reveal a recommendation for a
human to act on — they don't execute anything themselves." (Sets accurate expectations
before the click, avoiding a confusing moment.)

**Optional, if time allows:** walk one program's real multi-year arc (BRAVO's recovery
story or ECHO's correction) through Execution Monitoring's PY/CY tabs, showing the
variance table.
