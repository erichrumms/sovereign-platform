## PART I — STRATEGY

### 1. The Objective — WH-43's contingency is resolved, not just closed

**The core answer is unchanged and remains correct:** PPBE execution governance with
a deterministic compliance layer and a real, verified audit trail.

**What changed is that the plan's single load-bearing open claim is no longer open.**
v3.10 named WH-43 as "the platform's single most important reliability claim" and
declined to present it as resolved until it actually was. It now is — and the way it
resolved is worth stating plainly rather than smoothed into a simple "confirmed": the
live check found that the Session 71 fix, **shipped just eight days earlier**, had
itself introduced a one-item over-count. That was root-caused, reverted, and given a
permanent parity test — and the same parity-test pattern has since been extended to
5 of the platform's 7 Workspace tabs, turning one incident into standing, repeatable
coverage.

**A genuinely new body of evidence exists that v3.10 didn't have:** the platform now
tracks its own AI operating cost — real token counts, dollar estimates, failure
categorization, and response timing — at 14 of its 19 real live-call sites, live-
verified working end to end, including a real root-caused credential bug found and
fixed along the way. This is not a small addition; it answers a category of CTO
due-diligence question (operating cost visibility) this plan previously had no real
answer for.

**Demo-ready, precisely, as of this version:** everything v3.10 flagged as remaining
is closed. The WH-43 live check is closed, correctly, with evidence of the correction
kept in the repo rather than smoothed over. **The target of this plan is now Advance,
without contingency.**
